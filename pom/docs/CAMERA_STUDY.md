# Gameplay camera and scenery

The approved normal gameplay presentation is **Perspective + Follow**, **Orbit around puzzle**, and **Landscape**. The camera uses a 35° vertical field of view and a 20° base downward angle, follows Pom on both screen axes, and adjusts pitch when nearby pieces conceal him. Large puzzles retain the overview-to-Pom opening zoom. The cube selects three side bearings and two base heights; manual pan/zoom pauses following until valid gameplay resumes. Existing level completion saving remains enabled. Creator playtests use the same presentation with temporary progress.

**Settings → Gameplay → Piece highlight** offers **Pulsing light box** (the default, with an inward-only glow) and **Classic outline and tint**. The choice is saved without resetting other settings or progress. Reduced motion keeps the selected face steadily illuminated.

Open `?camera-lab=1`, for example `http://127.0.0.1:5173/?camera-lab=1`, for comparison controls. Only this explicit lab has disposable progress and visit-local controls; the creator's isolated playtest ignores the lab flag. The implementation history and dated review notes below document how the approved presentation was developed.

## Comparison controls

- Current oblique: original orthographic camera with depth-dependent geometry shear and existing cell squareness compensation.
- Gentle ortho: straight geometry, 20° side angle and 25° downward angle.
- Three-quarter: straight geometry, 35° side and downward angles.
- Front artwork: zero angles, for inspection. The ground is edge-on; increase the downward angle to play.
- Perspective + follow: 35° vertical field of view, 20° downward angle, orbit around the puzzle, horizontal and vertical Pom follow, and landscape scenery. The optional horizon-height lock starts off.
- Side angle can be adjusted from −40° to 40°, downward angle from 0° to 45°. The cube is hidden in this study. Side angle and the Orbit left / Center / Orbit right buttons use the same state. Left/right arrow keys change this angle in 5° steps, or track by 5% of puzzle width in sideways tracking mode.
- Use the puzzle selector for lessons, murals and Japan Challenges. Changing puzzles resets that puzzle normally; changing projection or scenery preserves the logical piece depths, Pom, selection and undo history.
- Pan and zoom work in the camera plane. Fit entire puzzle restores a conservative common scale reserved across the camera presets. Hide the panel on a phone for more space.

## Environment treatments

**Wall & ground set:** choose a fixed wall, a wall that turns with the horizontal viewing direction, or an open curved wall. Each is vertical and meets a real horizontal floor at 90°. The original background image above its measured ground anchor is applied to the wall; the opaque part of the foreground image below the anchor is applied to the floor. The fixed wall’s join changes slope with the camera. The turning wall keeps the join level. The curved wall’s join bows on screen; its center can stay at a fixed height. The front half of the curved set is open so it does not obscure the puzzle. Ground artwork remains world anchored.

**Landscape:** continuous ground plus an upright distant panorama, with hills, side trees and foreground plants at separate world depths. The panorama changes its horizontal bearing with the camera to keep its horizon level; the other scenery stays fixed. This is a stylized 2.5D study with a finite distant backdrop, not a complete 3D landscape. Meadow and Japan Spring use separate painted assets and thin cardboard standees. Other environments and the Previous artwork comparison retain the earlier scenery meshes. Trees and plants sit outside the playable floor bounds and are excluded from movement collision and pointer targeting.

The wall-set and Previous artwork comparisons reuse the earlier image files, which contain painted perspective and can stretch on a horizontal plane. The new Meadow/Japan Spring kits instead provide a top-down repeating ground, independent sky and hills, and transparent vegetation fronts. The artwork comparison selector preserves the current puzzle and camera state.

Background, ground artwork, landscape elements and the playable ground guide can be toggled independently. Ground shadows use the same piece-shadow system as the game. The guide shows the floor footprint; it does not claim every cell is currently reachable.

## Horizontal orbit and horizon checks

Side movement changes yaw only. With **Keep framing while orbiting** checked (the default), it preserves camera elevation, downward angle, orbit target, distance and orthographic scale, including manually panned or zoomed framing. Explicit camera presets, fitting, resizing and changing downward angle are separate operations.

**Hold horizon height during orbit** centers the turning backdrop or panorama on the actual camera orbit pivot and holds its horizontal distance from that pivot. This makes the projected ground join’s height independent of yaw. It also holds the curved wall’s center height. It cannot level a fixed wall’s join, so that option is disabled for the fixed-wall comparison. Unchecking it uses the puzzle’s original center instead, allowing height drift relative to a different orbit pivot.

The readout stays visible above the scrollable controls. It reports projected ground-join height as a percentage of the canvas and flat-join tilt. The dashed horizontal reference is drawn at the center height. Curved mode explicitly reports “curved”; its edges do not lie on that horizontal reference. Background objects are kept outside a conservative envelope around the puzzle so a turning wall cannot sweep through it. Each scenery treatment establishes its own horizon height; the lock holds that height during its orbit, rather than forcing all treatments to share a composition.

Orbiting leaves the logical puzzle and geometry unchanged. The lab skips rebuilding the board meshes during horizontal camera movement. The normal game retains its cube, now mapped to the approved perspective orbit.

## Perspective and player tracking

Choose **Perspective + follow** to try the Pushmo-inspired experiment. These values are a starting point for comparison, not a reconstruction of Pushmo’s exact camera settings. The field of view ranges from 25° to 60°. Changing it adjusts camera distance to preserve the vertical size at the focus plane; nearer and farther surfaces still change apparent size. Fitting remains a conservative shared board frame rather than a tight perspective fit.

**Camera movement** switches between orbiting and sideways tracking. Tracking translates the camera and focus together along world X, keeping yaw at zero and the downward angle fixed. The side-position slider ranges across ±60% of puzzle width; Track left/right use ±35%. This gives perspective parallax without turning the camera. Orbit mode instead exposes the side-angle slider and rotates around the focus.

Horizontal and vertical follow can be enabled independently in either straight camera mode. Manual panning and zooming pause follow for inspection. A valid movement tap, push/pull or undo resumes smooth follow toward the canvas center on the enabled axes, discarding any sideways tracking offset. Invalid movement taps leave the inspection frame alone. Follow uses the animated player pose and translates in the camera plane, so perspective depth and downward pitch do not leave Pom off-center. Camera movement eases over roughly 0.18 seconds. **Center on Pom** recenters immediately; Fit entire puzzle and explicit camera changes establish a stationary inspection frame until the next gameplay movement. Camera follow changes framing only and does not move world scenery or alter gameplay. Vertical follow can change the visible height of the finite backdrop join; the existing horizon-height option concerns horizontal orbit, not vertical movement.

The sidebar handles wheel, touch and pointer gestures locally so the engine’s document-level input handlers cannot cancel native panel scrolling. Its body scrolls independently, including over explanatory text. Header and horizon readout remain fixed. On narrow screens the expanded panel occupies 34% of viewport height; collapsing it returns that space to the board. Native form controls and keyboard scrolling remain available inside the panel.

## Data and implementation

Camera and scenery choices remain in page memory. The lab uses a disposable progress adapter and nonpersistent control settings; completing a built-in puzzle does not update the normal completion record. Level JSON, movement rules and puzzle collision stay unchanged.

`camera-lab.js` contains the temporary controls, `camera-scenery.js` the scenery geometry. The game routes visual depth shear through `depthSkew()` so prism meshes, markers, outlines, player animation, flag placement and pointer targeting use matching coordinates. Both straight camera modes remove the shear and Y compensation. Perspective uses the engine’s perspective lens and matching screen-to-world rays; the original orthographic lens remains available. Fitting, panning and zooming use the rotated camera basis in the lab.

## Selected front-face light box

The lab defaults to **Selected piece → Pulsing light box**, replacing the extra selection outline with a restrained color pulse on the selected front face. Its full fade-in/fade-out cycle is 1.4 seconds. The selected piece's top and sides retain their original steady painted shading. Reduced-motion preference produces a steady illuminated front. The controls offer the original outline/tint for comparison, strength 0–100% (default 65%), and **Inner glow**. These controls preserve camera framing and follow state. Ordinary gameplay defaults to the same inward light box, with the classic style available in Settings.

The current revision keeps the light entirely **inside** the selected face. A silhouette distance field fades the glow inward from the piece boundary, including concavities, without visible cell seams. Geometry covers exactly the selected face's cells; there is no outside halo, floor light pool, local point light or spill receiver on nearby pieces. A shallow front offset avoids overlap with the existing painted face. The field rebuilds only when selection, depth, level geometry or projection geometry changes, and its texture is released on replacement. Sunlight and ordinary cast shadows remain unchanged.

Color changes use OKLab perceptual color space instead of HSL. Chromatic pieces receive comparable perceptual color-distance changes with a small hue turn capped at 20° before gamut mapping, plus a brightness adjustment. This makes blue more readable without exaggerating yellow. White receives a cool tint and black lifts toward cool charcoal. On the actual tutorial blue and yellow palette colors, the peak perceptual delta at 65% strength is approximately 0.0617 and 0.0618 respectively. This is a numerical calibration, not a guarantee of identical perception on every display or background.

Browser review included a pulled blue Lesson 3 piece at a 35° side angle, its continuous internal gradient, steady top/side faces and unlit surrounding ground. Previous iterations also covered original/new selection comparison, strength endpoints, white/black pieces and irregular silhouettes. Physical-device performance and broader gameplay review remain outstanding.

## Adaptive Pom visibility

**Keep Pom visible automatically** is enabled in the lab's straight camera modes. The downward-angle slider remains the base angle; the readout underneath shows the actual angle during automatic adjustment. Ordinary gameplay uses the same automatic visibility behavior; the original-oblique comparison retains its old camera. Front artwork inspection below 5° does not run the automatic visibility check.

During gameplay, nine samples across Pom's core silhouette are checked against the solid puzzle cells, including resting cardboard backs. The solver first seeks the smallest steeper pitch that reveals at least eight samples, up to 65°. If a solid overhang prevents a steeper view from clearing him, it tries a shallower peek, down to 8°. If no angle completely clears him, it uses the angle that improves the visible sample count; it does not turn to a futile angle when visibility cannot improve. The existing through-wall character helper remains the fallback for fully closed geometry. Decorative scenery is excluded from the visibility calculation.

Camera-space compensation rotates the frame around Pom without changing his screen position, apparent size, FOV or side bearing. Follow remains independent. Adjustment eases over about 0.22 seconds; when the base view becomes clear, an 0.8-second hold and slower 0.65-second return reduce angle chatter. The solver checks at most about eight times per second and caches board boxes until piece depths change. Reduced motion uses the chosen angle directly. Looking more or less downward intentionally changes horizon height; horizontal level is retained.

Manual pan/zoom and explicit camera changes pause the automatic behavior for inspection until a valid gameplay movement resumes it. The checkbox can immediately enable the visibility study or ease back to the selected base angle when disabled. Angle changes update projection, picking and Pom's billboard during movement as well as while idle.

Review: a synthetic foreground-wall case changed from 20° / zero visible core samples to 58° / all samples visible. A synthetic overhang changed from 20° / zero samples to 12° / all visible. Camera-space position preservation was checked with an off-center Pom and nonzero side angle at three candidate pitches; projection differences were below 1e-15. In an isolated browser preview of Shooting Star, walking below the three-depth yellow arch changed the angle from 20° to approximately 12°, with base visibility 0/9 and adjusted visibility at least 8/9; Pom stayed at approximately 50% horizontally and vertically. These are geometry/browser checks, not catalog-wide or physical-device certification.

The Shooting Star browser check also toggled the feature off/on under the arch, then walked Pom back into the open: the base view returned to full sample visibility and eased back to 20°. Settling now snaps the final negligible angular remainder to the exact requested angle so the readout does not stop just short. Full game and restricted playtest builds passed; browser logs reported no runtime errors.

## Asset-ready presentation and build identification

Each level now waits behind a loading cover for its decoded scenery, vegetation textures and fonts. Only the newest requested level may reveal a scene or report a failure. Failed requests show a retry button; failed vegetation records can be discarded and reloaded, releasing partial GPU uploads. The cover remains through a composed frame before revealing the complete level and beginning its camera introduction. Back navigation cancels pending reveals. Creator playtests use the same gate.

The ordinary-game control deck overlays a constant-height canvas and is hidden as a whole during loading and the camera introduction, so there is no empty footer band or layout jump when controls return. Settings has independent wheel/touch handling so its build identifier is reachable. The identifier uses the GitHub workflow run number in deployment, a local timestamp otherwise, and includes the source revision and development marker where applicable.
